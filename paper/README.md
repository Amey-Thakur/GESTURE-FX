The paper behind this repository. It states the detection criterion as a theorem,
proves its invariances, derives every free parameter of the stylisation from a
noise statistic rather than fixing it by inspection, and reports the detector's
accuracy against a corpus with exact ground truth.

## The claim

For an open hand rotating about its long axis by $\theta$, the normalised
two-dimensional cross product of the projected palm edges factorises as
$s(\theta) = k(\theta)\cos\theta$ with $|k| > 0$ everywhere. The sign of $s$
therefore tracks the face presented to the camera, and $s$ vanishes exactly at
the edge-on configuration.

Detection is a zero crossing, which yields an **instant** rather than an
interval, and the zero lies between samples rather than on one. Interpolating it
localises the gesture to a fraction of the interval at which the hand is
observed, which a per-frame label cannot do.

## What was measured

Against 360 generated sequences with the crossing time known exactly:

| Quantity | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Flips detected | 95.0% |
| False positives | 0 in 240 near-miss sequences |
| Mean absolute localisation error | 6.7 ms, or 0.16 of a tracking interval |
| Median absolute error | 4.0 ms, or 0.10 of a tracking interval |
| Mean signed error | +0.8 ms |

The evaluation runs the shipped detector modules, draws from a seeded stream, and
is published as a page: **[reproduce it here](https://amey-thakur.github.io/GESTURE-FX/evaluation.html)**.
The source is [`Source Code/scripts/evaluation.ts`](../Source%20Code/scripts/evaluation.ts).

The corpus is generated rather than filmed because the crossing frame is not
observable in video to a precision finer than the error being measured. The
figures therefore bound the detector's own error and do not include the landmark
estimator's, which the paper states.
